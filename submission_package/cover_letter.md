# Cover Letter — Religion

Dear Editors,

We submit "Carry It Without Me: A Cyclical Theory of Normative Internalisation, Normative Release, and Religious Renewal" for consideration in *Religion*.

The paper asks a simple but consequential question that theories of religious transmission rarely follow through to their endpoint: what becomes of the religious representation once humans can carry the norm without it? Cognitive and evolutionary accounts explain how supernatural representations transmit and stabilise norms; norm theory explains how such norms become internalised. What remains under-theorised is not how religion transmits norms, nor how norms become internalised, but what happens to the religious carrier after that internalisation succeeds. We introduce *normative release* to describe the process by which a norm's reproduction becomes independent of the continued explicit salience of the representation that carried it.

The article is in direct conversation with scholarship published in *Religion*. Talmont-Kaminski and Shults's theory of religion as vestigial structure is the paper's nearest interlocutor, and we show the two models are complementary rather than rival—the survivors are inverted: in vestigialisation the structure persists while function fades; in normative release the norm persists while the scaffold recedes. We also place the proposal in dialogue with Anscombe's surviving "law conception," Nietzsche's shadows of God, and the religion-residue literature, and we derive a self-effacement paradox: a tradition may become least visible where a transmissive function has succeeded most completely. A norm-specific cyclical model reframes revival as selective reintegration of dormant normative repertoires rather than restoration.

The contribution is theoretical, calibrated, and falsifiable: we state six conditions under which the concept fails, and we make no claim that religion exists to carry norms or that decline is secretly triumph.

The manuscript is original, not under consideration elsewhere, and all cited works have been verified. We hope it finds a home in *Religion*'s ongoing conversation about what religion is and how it changes.

Sincerely,
